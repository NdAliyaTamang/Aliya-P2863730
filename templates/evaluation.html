Accuracy measures how often the predicted code matched the recorded target code across evaluated records. It is not a personal probability that a dress will fit.

Customers were grouped when separating development and held-out data. Most test dresses were seen during development, so these results mainly concern new customers choosing known dresses.

From form to prediction

  1. The backend validates the selected dress ID, bra-size category and numeric measurements.
  2. The exported helper prepares height, weight and bra-size values and converts the dress ID into the pipeline's dress-item category.
  3. The saved pipeline applies its fitted preprocessing and predicts one original dataset size code.

Submitting the form does not retrain or tune the model. Numeric validation currently checks for finite, positive measurements; it does not establish that an input is within the model's development-data range.

The prototype does not establish reduced commercial returns or measured environmental savings. Those outcomes require additional evidence.

Research success criterion

The project contract defines success as an accuracy improvement of at least five percentage points over a traditional size-chart baseline on the same test data.

Because that comparison remains unresolved, this criterion cannot currently be declared achieved.
